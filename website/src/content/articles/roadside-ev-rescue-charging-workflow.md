A mobile charger does not create a roadside-rescue service by itself. The service also needs dispatch information, a compatible connection, a safe operating procedure, a clear charging objective, and a handoff plan.

The following framework describes the workflow without pretending to be a customer case or a universal operating procedure. Final procedures must be developed for the selected equipment, vehicles, market, and roadside-assistance operation.

## 1. Receive and qualify the rescue request

The initial call should collect enough information to decide whether a mobile charging response is appropriate.

Useful inputs include:

- Vehicle make and model
- Vehicle location and access conditions
- Connector type
- Reported battery state and vehicle condition
- Whether the vehicle can enter a charging session
- Traffic, recovery, or site restrictions
- Intended destination after the charging response

The operator should distinguish an energy shortage from a vehicle fault. A mobile charger is not a substitute for recovery equipment when the vehicle cannot charge or cannot safely remain at the location.

## 2. Dispatch the configured mobile charging vehicle

The response vehicle must carry a system configured for the target connector and operating requirement. Dispatch planning should also consider route access, equipment transport, available stored energy, and the next opportunity to recharge the mobile system.

The system's published capacity and output power help qualify the equipment, but they do not replace an operating plan. The response team needs a defined process for pre-departure checks, equipment status, cable management, and communication with the stranded driver.

## 3. Establish the charging connection

At the scene, the operator confirms the vehicle identity, connector, physical condition, and positioning before connecting the charging system.

Connector availability depends on the selected product and configuration. A published list of connector standards should not be interpreted as universal compatibility with every EV. Confirm the vehicle-and-system combination during project configuration and include it in operator training.

The roadside location may also introduce risks unrelated to the charging equipment, including traffic exposure, restricted working space, weather, or unstable ground. These conditions belong in the service operator's site-assessment and safety procedure.

## 4. Deliver the defined emergency-energy handoff

The objective of a roadside charging response is normally operational: provide an agreed energy handoff so the vehicle can continue to an appropriate fixed charging or recovery location.

Avoid defining that handoff with an unsupported universal range or time promise. Actual charging behavior depends on the EV, battery state, temperature, voltage, connector, and configured equipment.

A better operating definition records:

- The target outcome for the response
- The session stop condition
- The maximum service time or energy policy set by the operator
- The destination or next action after disconnection
- The information recorded for service review

## 5. Complete the handoff and reset the system

After charging, the operator disconnects and secures the equipment according to the approved procedure. The vehicle either continues to the planned fixed charging location or follows the recovery path selected by the roadside-assistance team.

The mobile charging system then returns to service readiness. This can include checking remaining stored energy, scheduling recharge, inspecting cables and connectors, and recording the deployment.

## Plan the service around repeatability

A commercial rescue operation needs more than a successful demonstration. It needs a repeatable response model.

Before requesting a system configuration, document:

1. Service territory and typical response distance
2. Expected calls per operating period
3. Target vehicle and connector mix
4. Intended emergency-energy handoff
5. Recharge source and available turnaround time
6. Transport and mounting approach
7. Operator procedure and training responsibility
8. Data or charging-management requirements

These inputs help connect product selection to the actual service design.
